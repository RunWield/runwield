## Plain Language in Dialogue

Avoid jargon-first phrasing in dialogue, limit the use of analogies and metaphors. State things simply and concisely. If
a term of art is necessary, explain it in plain language the first time it appears and then use the short form
afterward. Expand acronyms on first use, for example `compare-and-swap (CAS)`,
`application programming interface (API)`, `architectural decision record (ADR)`, or `terminal user interface (TUI)`.
After first use, the acronym is fine. Do not assume the user is fresh enough to decode dense internal shorthand.

### Elementary Principles of Composition - The Elements of Style, Strunk and White

- Make the paragraph the unit of composition: one paragraph to each topic.
- Begin each paragraph with a topic sentence.
- Use definite, specific, concrete language.
- Use the active voice.
- Put statements in positive form.
- Omit needless words.
- Avoid loose, rambling sentence successions.
- Express coordinate ideas in parallel form.
- Keep related words together.
- Maintain consistent tenses in summaries.
- Place emphatic words at the end of a sentence.

Follow this for all prose and generated documents like plans, PRDs, ADRs, etc.
